# Positioning: LLM-Guided Socratic Hint Generation with Student-State Awareness

Synthesized from the five verified subarea literature files (`01`–`05`) and `references.bib` (32 verified entries). Every citation key below exists in `references.bib`.

**Search protocol note:** This synthesis is a scoping-level literature pass, not an exhaustive systematic review. Sources searched were arXiv, ACL Anthology, AIED/EDM/L@S proceedings, and Google Scholar, targeting ~6-8 papers per subarea biased toward highly-cited and recent (2022-2026) work plus 1-2 foundational/canonical papers per subarea. Negative claims below ("no work found...", "none of them...") should be read as bounded by this search — *within the surveyed literature* — not as an absolute claim that no such work exists anywhere. A full systematic review with documented search strings and inclusion/exclusion criteria would be needed to make an unqualified novelty claim; that is out of scope for this scoping pass.

## What's established

**ITS effectiveness has a well-quantified baseline.** `vanlehn2011relative`'s meta-analysis puts step-based/substep-based ITS at d ≈ 0.76 against a no-tutoring baseline, closing most of the gap to human tutors (d ≈ 0.79, measured against the same no-tutoring baseline). `kulik2016effectiveness`'s independent meta-analysis of 50 evaluations reports a median of d ≈ 0.66 across ITS of all kinds relative to *conventional instruction* — a different estimand with a different comparison baseline, so the two figures are not a single range, but they converge on the same conclusion that ITS effectiveness approaches human-tutor levels. The mechanism `vanlehn2011relative` identifies as driving the ITS effect — fine-grained, step-level interaction rather than answer-checking — motivates hint-level (not just final-answer) intervention, which is this thesis's level of operation.

**Socratic/scaffolding pedagogy is theoretically well-formalized, and has non-LLM computational precedent.** `stevens1977goal` gives one of the earliest formal models of Socratic dialogue moves; `wood1976role` defines scaffolding itself; `graesser2004autotutor`'s pump→hint→prompt→assertion taxonomy is a widely-cited structured hint vocabulary. Critically, `rivers2017datadriven` shows a full **non-LLM, data-driven hint generator** (ITAP) that abstracts student program state and generates next-step hints from it — proving the "state-conditioned hint" concept works, but via explicit graph/path abstraction over code, not language understanding.

**LLM-generated Socratic questioning exists, but in narrow domains without explicit, live state adaptation.** The underlying premise — that LLM-generated hints can be pedagogically effective at all — has direct causal support: `pardos2024chatgpt`'s randomized efficacy study (N=274, 3×4 design across four algebra/statistics topics) found ChatGPT-generated help produced significant learning gains over a no-help control and *no* significant difference from human-tutor-authored help. That is the strongest causal evidence in this bibliography for the thesis's basic premise, though the help it tested was pre-authored per problem rather than generated live and adapted to a student's current state. On the Socratic-generation technique specifically, `shridhar2022automatic` (math word problems) and `alhossami2023socratic` (programming debugging) are the nearest neighbours: both use LLMs to generate Socratic-style questions rather than answers. Neither incorporates an explicit, tracked student-state representation that adapts hint content in real time. `shridhar2022automatic`'s own findings show generated questions can help *or hurt* depending on problem difficulty — the paper reports difficulty as a moderator without analysing the mechanism; lack of adaptation to the student's current understanding is one candidate explanation, and one this thesis is positioned to test directly. `macina2023mathdial` supplies independent, converging evidence for the same underlying problem: LLMs prompted to tutor tend to give away answers prematurely rather than scaffold, which is exactly the failure mode a live student-state signal should let a generator avoid. `sonkar2023class` (CLASS/SPOCK) shows a full learning-science-grounded design framework for building LLM-powered ITS around scaffolding principles, but its two-dataset design (scaffolding + conversational) is a static training-data structure, not a mechanism for tracking an individual student's state across a live session.

`liu2024socraticlm` (SocraticLM) is the closest competitor overall — nearest not on any single technique but on the whole system-level ambition of this thesis — and required direct verification rather than a surface read: the paper's "multi-agent Dean-Teacher-Student pipeline" is a **dataset-construction** pipeline, not the deployed system — all three agents are GPT-4 instances used only to generate the SocraTeach training set, after which a single model (ChatGLM3-6b) is fine-tuned on that data to become the deployed SocraticLM. The "Student" agent in that pipeline is a simulated learner whose "cognitive state" is a **fixed persona re-sampled independently on each reply** from six hand-specified profiles (weak in one of five dimensions, or strong in all) — not an inferred, per-turn-updated state that accumulates evidence across the dialogue. SocraticLM therefore does exactly what this thesis's related work needs a contrast case to do: it demonstrates that a *student cognitive-state taxonomy* is a viable design input for LLM Socratic generation, while leaving untouched the specific mechanism this thesis targets — inferring that state live from the dialogue itself and conditioning each hint on the current inferred state, evaluated across turns rather than as single-response quality.

**Student-state modeling has moved from structured to language-native, but is evaluated in isolation from hint generation.** The lineage runs from `corbett1995knowledge` (BKT) → `piech2015deep` (DKT) → `pandey2019selfattentive` / `ghosh2020contextaware` (attention-based) → `scarlatos2025exploring`, which has an LLM read **raw tutor-student dialogue transcripts** and infer per-skill mastery from them rather than from structured correct/incorrect logs. `jung2024clst` is a complementary rather than equivalent result: it reformulates conventional knowledge-tracing interaction data as a natural-language generation task, showing that an LLM's world knowledge can substitute for large amounts of per-student interaction history under cold-start conditions — not that state can be read off free-form dialogue. Together they establish that LLM-based state inference is feasible both from dialogue (Scarlatos et al.) and from thin interaction histories (Jung et al.).

In the post-BKT papers of that lineage (`piech2015deep`, `pandey2019selfattentive`, `ghosh2020contextaware`, `scarlatos2025exploring`, `jung2024clst`), state inference is evaluated as a **standalone prediction task** (does the model predict the next answer correctly?), with no pedagogical action taken on the inferred state. `corbett1995knowledge` is a partial precedent in the other direction: BKT's per-skill mastery estimates were shown within the ACT Programming Tutor to drive individualized *problem selection*. But problem selection is a coarse-grained pedagogical action — choosing what to work on next — not generation of the next conversational turn. Narrowing the claim to what the sources support: within the surveyed literature, none of these papers feed an inferred student state into **hint or utterance generation** specifically. `dmello2012autotutor` further shows "student state" plausibly extends beyond knowledge mastery to affect/engagement, widening what "state-aware" could mean. `cho2024systematic`'s survey names a related open problem — KT-LLM integration's dependence on structured datasets and underuse of richer contextual/textual student data — which is adjacent to, though not identical with, the closed-loop gap argued here.

**Evaluation methodology for tutoring/hint quality is mature but generic.** LLM-as-judge is validated (`zheng2023judging`) and applied to tutoring specifically via `tack2022aiteachertest`'s multi-dimension comparative protocol, `macina2025mathtutorbench`'s reward-model benchmark (which finds problem-solving skill does *not* predict teaching skill), `scarlatos2025training`'s outcome+rubric DPO combination, and `maurya2025unifying`'s eight-dimension taxonomy with the MRBench dataset. `kestin2025aitutoring` shows a full classroom RCT design is viable and can show AI tutoring benefits over active-learning classrooms. None of these evaluation frameworks include a dimension specifically measuring whether a hint's content or specificity is *appropriately adapted to an assessed student state* — they measure general pedagogical soundness, not state-conditioned appropriateness.

## The gap

Within the surveyed literature (see the search protocol note above), two lines of work run in parallel without meeting. One line (`shridhar2022automatic`, `alhossami2023socratic`, `sonkar2023class`, `liu2024socraticlm`) generates LLM-based Socratic hints/questions but treats the student as effectively state-less at generation time — no explicit, carried-forward representation of what the student currently knows or misunderstands conditions what hint comes next; SocraticLM's fixed-persona student profile — re-sampled independently on each reply, rather than carried forward across the dialogue — is the closest approach to this and is verified to fall short of it (see above). The other line shows LLM-based state inference is feasible: `scarlatos2025exploring` infers state directly from tutor-student dialogue, `jung2024clst` shows LLM world knowledge can stand in for a thin interaction history, and the DKT lineage behind them supplies the sequential-state formalism. But that inference is evaluated in isolation as next-answer prediction, never fed forward into hint or utterance generation (with `corbett1995knowledge`'s BKT-driven problem selection as the partial, coarser-grained precedent noted above). `rivers2017datadriven` proves the state→hint pipeline works in principle, but only for a structured, non-linguistic domain (program-state graphs), not for open dialogue. And within `05-evaluation-methodology.md`, no evaluation framework found scores hints on state-appropriateness specifically — `macina2025mathtutorbench` and `maurya2025unifying` score general pedagogical quality, which is a necessary but not sufficient proxy for "did this hint match what the student needed right now."

## This thesis's contribution

This thesis closes that loop: an LLM-guided Socratic hint generator that explicitly infers student state from ongoing tutoring dialogue (extending the dialogue-based state inference `scarlatos2025exploring` shows to be feasible from a prediction target into a live pedagogical signal, and drawing on `jung2024clst` for the separate result that LLM world knowledge can carry state estimation when per-student interaction history is thin — as it necessarily is early in any single tutoring session) and uses that inferred state to condition hint content and specificity (extending the Socratic-generation techniques of `shridhar2022automatic` and `alhossami2023socratic` with the adaptive mechanism they lack, in the spirit of what `rivers2017datadriven` did non-linguistically for code). Evaluation should extend existing tutoring-quality rubrics (`macina2025mathtutorbench`, `maurya2025unifying`) with a state-adaptivity dimension — not just "is this a good Socratic hint," but "is this the Socratic hint this specific student, at this specific inferred state, needed."
