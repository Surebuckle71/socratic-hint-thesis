# Positioning: LLM-Guided Socratic Hint Generation with Student-State Awareness

Sources searched were arXiv, ACL Anthology, AIED/EDM/L@S proceedings, and Google Scholar, targeting ~6-8 papers per subarea biased toward highly-cited and recent (2022-2026) work plus 1-2 foundational/canonical papers per subarea. ## What's established

**ITS effectiveness has a well-quantified baseline.** Two independent meta-analyses (`vanlehn2011relative`, `kulik2016effectiveness`) put step/substep-based ITS effect sizes at d ≈ 0.66–0.76, closing most of the gap to human tutors (d ≈ 0.79). The mechanism identified as driving this — fine-grained, step-level interaction rather than answer-checking — motivates hint-level (not just final-answer) intervention, which is this thesis's level of operation.

**Socratic/scaffolding pedagogy is theoretically well-formalized, and has non-LLM computational precedent.** `stevens1977goal` gives one of the earliest formal models of Socratic dialogue moves; `wood1976role` defines scaffolding itself; `graesser2004autotutor`'s pump→hint→prompt→assertion taxonomy is a widely-cited structured hint vocabulary. Critically, `rivers2017datadriven` shows a full **non-LLM, data-driven hint generator** (ITAP) that abstracts student program state and generates next-step hints from it — proving the "state-conditioned hint" concept works, but via explicit graph/path abstraction over code, not language understanding.

**LLM-generated Socratic questioning exists, but in narrow domains without explicit, live state adaptation.** `shridhar2022automatic` (math word problems) and `alhossami2023socratic` (programming debugging) are the two closest neighbors to this thesis's core technique: both use LLMs to generate Socratic-style questions rather than answers. Neither incorporates an explicit, tracked student-state representation that adapts hint content in real time. `shridhar2022automatic`'s own findings show generated questions can help *or hurt* depending on problem difficulty — the paper reports difficulty as a moderator without analysing the mechanism; lack of adaptation to the student's current understanding is one candidate explanation, and one this thesis is positioned to test directly. `macina2023mathdial` supplies independent, converging evidence for the same underlying problem: LLMs prompted to tutor tend to give away answers prematurely rather than scaffold, which is exactly the failure mode a live student-state signal should let a generator avoid. `sonkar2023class` (CLASS/SPOCK) shows a full learning-science-grounded design framework for building LLM-powered ITS around scaffolding principles, but its two-dataset design (scaffolding + conversational) is a static training-data structure, not a mechanism for tracking an individual student's state across a live session.

`liu2024socraticlm` (SocraticLM) is the closest competitor in the entire bibliography and required direct verification rather than a surface read: the paper's "multi-agent Dean-Teacher-Student pipeline" is a **dataset-construction** pipeline, not the deployed system — all three agents are GPT-4 instances used only to generate the SocraTeach training set, after which a single model (ChatGLM3-6b) is fine-tuned on that data to become the deployed SocraticLM. The "Student" agent in that pipeline is a simulated learner whose "cognitive state" is a **fixed persona sampled once per synthetic dialogue** from six hand-specified profiles (weak in one of five dimensions, or strong in all) — not an inferred, per-turn-updated state. SocraticLM therefore does exactly what this thesis's related work needs a contrast case to do: it demonstrates that a *student cognitive-state taxonomy* is a viable design input for LLM Socratic generation, while leaving untouched the specific mechanism this thesis targets — inferring that state live from the dialogue itself and conditioning each hint on the current inferred state, evaluated across turns rather than as single-response quality.

**Student-state modeling has moved from structured to language-native, but is evaluated in isolation from hint generation.** The lineage from `corbett1995knowledge` (BKT) → `piech2015deep` (DKT) → `pandey2019selfattentive` / `ghosh2020contextaware` (attention-based) → `scarlatos2025exploring` and `jung2024clst` (LLMs inferring state directly from raw dialogue, not structured correct/incorrect logs) demonstrates that LLM-based state inference from free-form tutoring dialogue is now feasible and competitive. But in every one of these papers, state inference is evaluated as a **standalone prediction task** (does the model predict the next answer correctly?) — none of them close the loop by using the inferred state to generate the next pedagogical action. `dmello2012autotutor` further shows "student state" plausibly extends beyond knowledge mastery to affect/engagement, widening what "state-aware" could mean. `cho2024systematic`'s survey explicitly names this as an open problem: KT-LLM integration underuses richer contextual/textual student data.

**Evaluation methodology for tutoring/hint quality is mature but generic.** LLM-as-judge is validated (`zheng2023judging`) and applied to tutoring specifically via `tack2022aiteachertest`'s multi-dimension comparative protocol, `macina2025mathtutorbench`'s reward-model benchmark (which finds problem-solving skill does *not* predict teaching skill), `scarlatos2025training`'s outcome+rubric DPO combination, and `maurya2025unifying`'s eight-dimension taxonomy with the MRBench dataset. `kestin2025aitutoring` shows a full classroom RCT design is viable and can show AI tutoring benefits over active-learning classrooms. None of these evaluation frameworks include a dimension specifically measuring whether a hint's content or specificity is *appropriately adapted to an assessed student state* — they measure general pedagogical soundness, not state-conditioned appropriateness.

## The gap

Within the surveyed literature (see the search protocol note above), two lines of work run in parallel without meeting. One line (`shridhar2022automatic`, `alhossami2023socratic`, `sonkar2023class`, `liu2024socraticlm`) generates LLM-based Socratic hints/questions but treats the student as effectively state-less at generation time — no explicit, carried-forward representation of what the student currently knows or misunderstands conditions what hint comes next; SocraticLM's fixed-persona student profile is the closest approach to this and is verified to fall short of it (see above). The other line (`scarlatos2025exploring`, `jung2024clst`, and the DKT lineage behind them) shows LLMs can infer a usable student-state representation directly from dialogue, but only evaluates that inference in isolation, never feeding it back into pedagogical action generation. `rivers2017datadriven` proves the state→hint pipeline works in principle, but only for a structured, non-linguistic domain (program-state graphs), not for open dialogue. And within `05-evaluation-methodology.md`, no evaluation framework found scores hints on state-appropriateness specifically — `macina2025mathtutorbench` and `maurya2025unifying` score general pedagogical quality, which is a necessary but not sufficient proxy for "did this hint match what the student needed right now."

## This thesis's contribution

This thesis closes that loop: an LLM-guided Socratic hint generator that explicitly infers student state from ongoing tutoring dialogue (extending the feasibility shown by `scarlatos2025exploring` and `jung2024clst` from prediction into a live pedagogical signal) and uses that inferred state to condition hint content and specificity (extending the Socratic-generation techniques of `shridhar2022automatic` and `alhossami2023socratic` with the adaptive mechanism they lack, in the spirit of what `rivers2017datadriven` did non-linguistically for code). Evaluation should extend existing tutoring-quality rubrics (`macina2025mathtutorbench`, `maurya2025unifying`) with a state-adaptivity dimension — not just "is this a good Socratic hint," but "is this the Socratic hint this specific student, at this specific inferred state, needed."
