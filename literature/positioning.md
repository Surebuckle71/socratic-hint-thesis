# Positioning: LLM-Guided Socratic Hint Generation with Student-State Awareness

Synthesized from the five verified subarea literature files (`01`–`05`) and `references.bib` (32 verified entries). Every citation key below exists in `references.bib`.

## What's established

**ITS effectiveness has a well-quantified baseline.** Two independent meta-analyses (`vanlehn2011relative`, `kulik2016effectiveness`) put step/substep-based ITS effect sizes at d ≈ 0.66–0.76, closing most of the gap to human tutors (d ≈ 0.79). The mechanism identified as driving this — fine-grained, step-level interaction rather than answer-checking — motivates hint-level (not just final-answer) intervention, which is this thesis's level of operation.

**Socratic/scaffolding pedagogy is theoretically well-formalized, and has non-LLM computational precedent.** `stevens1977goal` gives one of the earliest formal models of Socratic dialogue moves; `wood1976role` defines scaffolding itself; `graesser2004autotutor`'s pump→hint→prompt→assertion taxonomy is a widely-cited structured hint vocabulary. Critically, `rivers2017datadriven` shows a full **non-LLM, data-driven hint generator** (ITAP) that abstracts student program state and generates next-step hints from it — proving the "state-conditioned hint" concept works, but via explicit graph/path abstraction over code, not language understanding.

**LLM-generated Socratic questioning exists, but in narrow domains without explicit state adaptation.** `shridhar2022automatic` (math word problems) and `alhossami2023socratic` (programming debugging) are the two closest neighbors to this thesis's core technique: both use LLMs to generate Socratic-style questions rather than answers. Neither incorporates an explicit, tracked student-state representation that adapts hint content — `shridhar2022automatic`'s own findings show generated questions can help *or hurt* depending on problem difficulty, an effect the paper attributes to the *absence* of adaptation to what the student currently understands. `liu2024socraticlm` (SocraticLM) is the most architecturally ambitious LLM Socratic system found (multi-agent Dean-Teacher-Student), but its "Thought-Provoking" paradigm is not built around an explicit, separately-modeled student-state component either.

**Student-state modeling has moved from structured to language-native, but is evaluated in isolation from hint generation.** The lineage from `corbett1995knowledge` (BKT) → `piech2015deep` (DKT) → `pandey2019selfattentive` / `ghosh2020contextaware` (attention-based) → `scarlatos2025exploring` and `jung2024clst` (LLMs inferring state directly from raw dialogue, not structured correct/incorrect logs) demonstrates that LLM-based state inference from free-form tutoring dialogue is now feasible and competitive. But in every one of these papers, state inference is evaluated as a **standalone prediction task** (does the model predict the next answer correctly?) — none of them close the loop by using the inferred state to generate the next pedagogical action. `dmello2012autotutor` further shows "student state" plausibly extends beyond knowledge mastery to affect/engagement, widening what "state-aware" could mean. `cho2024systematic`'s survey explicitly names this as an open problem: KT-LLM integration underuses richer contextual/dialogue signals relative to what downstream pedagogical use could exploit.

**Evaluation methodology for tutoring/hint quality is mature but generic.** LLM-as-judge is validated (`zheng2023judging`) and applied to tutoring specifically via `tack2022aiteachertest`'s multi-dimension comparative protocol, `macina2025mathtutorbench`'s reward-model benchmark (which finds problem-solving skill does *not* predict teaching skill), `scarlatos2025training`'s outcome+rubric DPO combination, and `maurya2025unifying`'s eight-dimension taxonomy with the MRBench dataset. `kestin2025aitutoring` shows a full classroom RCT design is viable and can show AI tutoring benefits over active-learning classrooms. None of these evaluation frameworks include a dimension specifically measuring whether a hint's content or specificity is *appropriately adapted to an assessed student state* — they measure general pedagogical soundness, not state-conditioned appropriateness.

## The gap

Two literatures run in parallel without meeting. One line (`shridhar2022automatic`, `alhossami2023socratic`, `liu2024socraticlm`) generates LLM-based Socratic hints/questions but treats the student as effectively state-less at generation time — no explicit, carried-forward representation of what the student currently knows or misunderstands conditions what hint comes next. The other line (`scarlatos2025exploring`, `jung2024clst`, and the DKT lineage behind them) shows LLMs can infer a usable student-state representation directly from dialogue, but only evaluates that inference in isolation, never feeding it back into pedagogical action generation. `rivers2017datadriven` proves the state→hint pipeline works in principle, but only for a structured, non-linguistic domain (program-state graphs), not for open dialogue. And no evaluation framework in `05-evaluation-methodology.md` scores hints on state-appropriateness specifically — `macina2025mathtutorbench` and `maurya2025unifying` score general pedagogical quality, which is a necessary but not sufficient proxy for "did this hint match what the student needed right now."

## This thesis's contribution

This thesis closes that loop: an LLM-guided Socratic hint generator that explicitly infers student state from ongoing tutoring dialogue (extending the feasibility shown by `scarlatos2025exploring` and `jung2024clst` from prediction into a live pedagogical signal) and uses that inferred state to condition hint content and specificity (extending the Socratic-generation techniques of `shridhar2022automatic` and `alhossami2023socratic` with the adaptive mechanism they lack, in the spirit of what `rivers2017datadriven` did non-linguistically for code). Evaluation should extend existing tutoring-quality rubrics (`macina2025mathtutorbench`, `maurya2025unifying`) with a state-adaptivity dimension — not just "is this a good Socratic hint," but "is this the Socratic hint this specific student, at this specific inferred state, needed."
